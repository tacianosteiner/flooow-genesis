## Thesis and institutional boundary

Working name: **FLOOOW Opportunity Capital Foundry**. Flooow could evolve from
economic truth → decision toward:

```text
economic truth → opportunity detection → qualification → validation
  → capital thesis → capital gates → governed execution → outcome → learning
```

Economic truth remains governed institutional reality. Opportunity intelligence
consumes it and produces hypotheses, uncertainty, scenarios and proposals.
Policy and authorized operators separately decide whether execution may occur.
Research confidence, model output or a capital gate does not itself grant authority.
This follows the existing [MGI convergence boundary](../roadmap/MGI-GENESIS-CONVERGENCE.md).
It introduces no dependency into Package 0090 or the current Room sequence.

The long-term aspiration is system-discovered opportunity, rather than requiring
the user to invent every product idea. Inputs might combine market observations,
economic truth, product and competitor intelligence, creator/content evidence,
supplier evidence and capital economics. The resulting output must expose
what must be true, source quality, uncertainty and the next information purchase.

Illustrative future output: “Technical off-road footwear may be underserved at
price/value point X; Y potentially compatible factories and category overlap Z
need qualification. Twelve hypothetical high-fit creators suggest a distribution
test. Durability and return rate remain uncertainties; propose R$N of prototype /
creator tests before inventory.” X/Y/Z/N and the creator count are scenario
placeholders, not system observations, recommendations or execution authority.

Each proposed value needs its evidence type, source/as-of time, scope, assumptions,
uncertainty and reconciliation path. Missing cost, evidence or identity remains
unknown. It is never silently converted to zero, a match or a complete opportunity.
Projected margin must expose landed cost, fees, taxes, freight, acquisition,
returns, warranty, stock loss and working-capital assumptions as applicable;
gross margin alone is insufficient to compare contribution and downside.

## Existing-brand expansion laboratory: Redmoto

Hypothetical question: if Redmoto had **R$1,000,000 of new capital**, which
deployment would maximize risk-adjusted economic value? This is a research
question, not an allocation recommendation or assertion that funds exist.

Compare core inventory, new SKU, adjacent category, new product development,
new brand, acquisition, distribution opportunity, marketing, channel expansion
and other discovered opportunities. Include holding capital when information
quality is inadequate. A new product earns no preference merely because it is new.

Use a common horizon and distinguish incremental cash contribution, capital tied
up, timing, downside, reversibility, concentration, execution capacity and the
information gained. Compare forecasts with the same evidence standards, rather
than privileging a novel brand with optimistic demand assumptions.

## New-brand creation requires multiple evidence families

Illustrative hypothesis: outdoor/off-road lifestyle → technical riding boots.
Claims of market growth, expensive international brands, weak domestic alternatives,
durability complaints and price/value whitespace are **unverified scenario inputs**.
They are insufficient to create a brand or release production capital.

| Evidence family | What a future qualification must establish |
|---|---|
| MARKET | Demand, geography, growth interpretation, seasonality and representative evidence |
| PRODUCT | Actual unmet problem, fit, durability and differentiated performance |
| ECONOMICS | Contribution, returns/warranty exposure and sensitivity to price/cost |
| SUPPLY | Qualified factories, prototype capability, capacity, quality, MOQ and landed cost |
| DISTRIBUTION | A plausible measured customer path, rather than summed followers |
| AUDIENCE | Relevant, reachable customers, overlap and willingness to act/pay |
| CREATOR | Category authority, decomposed fit, cost and observed actionability |
| CHANNEL | Feasibility, customer acquisition, channel constraints and economics |
| CAPITAL | Tranches, working-capital cycle, maximum loss and capital alternatives |
| EXECUTION | Operator capability, delivery plan and constraints |
| RISK | Failure modes, uncertainty, downside, evidence gaps and kill rules |

## Capital gates and pre-launch distribution proof

Capital is earned by evidence. These gates are a research design, not approvals:

| Gate | Required evidence | Capital | Maximum loss | Success condition | Kill rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G0 Research | Typed problem/demand signals, alternatives and source quality | TBD; proposed research cap | TBD; bounded research loss | A falsifiable thesis and valuable next test | Unsupported gap or insufficient expected information value |
| G1 Design | Product requirements, supplier feasibility, target economics | TBD | TBD | Testable design with plausible contribution | Infeasible design or no defensible differentiation |
| G2 Prototype | Physical quality/fit/durability tests and revised costing | TBD; staged prototypes | TBD | Predetermined product criteria met | Safety/quality/fit/cost failure |
| G3 Creator validation | Qualified cluster, legitimate creator interest, sample-test plan | TBD; capped activation | TBD | Credible category fit and observable test commitments | No relevant interest or unjustifiable test cost |
| G4 Audience/market validation | Content response, landing page, waitlist/intent and appropriately governed reservation tests | TBD | TBD | Predetermined audience/economic thresholds met | Weak qualified demand or acquisition economics |
| G5 Pilot inventory | Small-batch sales, realized contribution, delivery and returns | TBD; bounded pilot | TBD | Reconciled contribution and viable working-capital cycle | Returns/quality/contribution or cash-cycle breach |
| G6 Scale | Repeatable cohort/channel outcomes, supply reliability and downside cases | TBD; tranches | TBD | Incremental capital earns approved economics | Economics deteriorate or capacity/risk limits breached |

TBD is an information gap, not permission. Every transition requires a separately
approved evidence definition, amount, maximum loss, success condition and kill rule.
No automated gate engine or execution authority is implemented here.

Preserve the suggested validation sequence: identify creator cluster → validate
interest → prototype → distribute samples to selected creators → measure content
response → capture waitlist/intent → test landing page → consider reservation/deposit
only after legal/commercial qualification → test small batch → consider scale.
Waitlists, content response and reservations remain distinct from fulfilled sales.

## Capital opportunity frontier

Compare **marginal capital return**, rather than only “this project requires R$1M”.
Illustrative incremental tranches are R$25k research, R$60k design/prototype,
R$100k creator/audience validation, R$200k pilot and R$600k inventory/launch.
Their illustrative total is R$985k; they are neither an approved budget nor a
complete use of the hypothetical R$1M. No amount automatically maps to approval
of an individual gate. Release a tranche only when prior evidence earns it.

For each proposed increment, compare incremental contribution/cash timing,
downside, reversibility and information value against core Redmoto inventory,
boots, another category, a new brand, acquisition and other discovered opportunities.
Maintain scenario ranges and correlated risks; a single unexplained expected-return
score cannot replace the evidence or disclose how uncertainty affects ranking.

## Distribution as a prospective asset

Product gap + supplier advantage + brand thesis + creator network + owned customer
base + marketplace channel might produce a defensible distribution advantage.
This is a superiority hypothesis to test, not a proven moat. Evaluate retention,
repeat acquisition, concentration, channel dependency, content reuse rights and
durability alongside product economics.

## Earned predictability and learning

Future statements must distinguish OBSERVED, INFERRED, ESTIMATED, SIMULATED,
CONTRACTED and ACTUAL, with provenance and uncertainty. A platform-displayed count
is an observation of that display, not verified human reach. A creator commitment
is a contract fact, not delivered views or sales. Predictions cannot promise reach;
literal contracted/delivered observations must retain their exact scope.

After separately authorized launches: creator → content → views → clicks →
conversion → CAC → returns → contribution → repeat purchase. Reconcile orders,
refunds and costs with canonical economic truth before treating outcomes as actual.
Separate attribution assumptions from causal claims; retain failed forecasts and
unmatched outcomes rather than suppressing them.

Preserve a CAPITAL LEARNING LOOP, DISTRIBUTION LEARNING LOOP and PRODUCT LEARNING
LOOP: which creators sell, audiences convert, categories work, forecasts fail,
suppliers outperform, brand extensions work and capital gates predict success.
Learning may improve future proposals; broader autonomy requires measured
calibration, downside, reversibility, policy compliance and outcome history.
