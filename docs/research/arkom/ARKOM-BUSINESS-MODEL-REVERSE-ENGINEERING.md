# FLOOOW — ARKOM Business Model Reverse Engineering

Status: STRATEGIC RESEARCH
Captured: 2026-10-10
Room critical path impact: NONE
Implementation authority: NONE

## Public positioning

ARKOM publicly positions itself as a B2B AI platform focused on operational implementation inside established companies.

Observed public offer:
- ready-made AI systems;
- white-label systems;
- implementation guidance;
- corporate AI education;
- group and private mentoring;
- custom solutions;
- an agent/system builder called Genesis.

Public functional categories include sales, finance, support, HR, marketing, legal, management and product.

## ICP

ARKOM's own implementation guide targets companies that already have teams and operating processes. Its public materials reference businesses already generating meaningful revenue; a press article states focus on firms above R$1 million in monthly revenue, while a blog guide uses above R$1 million/year as a broader content audience. These should not be treated as the same qualification threshold.

## Pricing observed

Public plans observed:
- Basic: R$2,157/month, annual contract, up to 3 users.
- Tração: R$3,153/month, annual contract, up to 12 users.
- Enterprise: R$5,157/month, annual contract, unlimited users.

The plans bundle software + systems + education + mentoring + implementation assistance.

This means ARKOM is not publicly packaged as pure SaaS. It is closer to:

SOFTWARE
+ IMPLEMENTATION METHOD
+ EDUCATION
+ COMMUNITY
+ ADVISORY
+ CUSTOMIZATION

## ARR target

A public press article reports ARKOM's own target of R$50M ARR in 12–18 months. This is a company projection, not independently audited.

At current published pricing, a rough simplified steady-state equivalent would require on the order of hundreds to low-thousands of annual customers depending on plan mix. This is only arithmetic inference and ignores discounts, services, custom enterprise revenue, churn and expansion.

## Implementation doctrine

ARKOM's public implementation method is strong and commercially clear:

PROCESS FIRST
-> BASELINE
-> SMALL PILOT
-> HUMAN OWNER
-> MEASURE ROI
-> SCALE

Their published 90-day structure:
- days 1–15: choose process, baseline hours/cost, organize knowledge;
- days 16–45: small implementation pilot with human review;
- days 46–75: adjust and expand within the function;
- days 76–90: close measured result and decide whether to scale.

This is one of the strongest elements worth studying.

## Value proposition

ARKOM sells "installed capacity", not model access.

Commercial framing:
- do more without growing headcount proportionally;
- remove repetitive work;
- deploy AI into operational processes;
- prove value in hours saved or revenue;
- reduce implementation friction.

This is easier for mainstream businesses to buy than abstract "AI transformation".

## Public case pattern

ARKOM showcases operational outcomes such as:
- SDR / pre-sales automation;
- support cost reduction;
- faster proposal creation;
- onboarding acceleration;
- checkout recovery;
- faster budgeting;
- 24/7 support.

Many results are customer-reported and the site explicitly disclaims guaranteed outcomes.

## Likely commercial flywheel

Inference from public materials:

CONTENT / THIAGO FINCH DISTRIBUTION
-> DIAGNOSTIC
-> ANNUAL PLATFORM CONTRACT
-> READY SYSTEMS
-> MENTORING / IMPLEMENTATION
-> FIRST QUICK WIN
-> MORE FUNCTIONS
-> RETENTION / EXPANSION

This is an inference, not a disclosed internal funnel.

## Strongest observed strengths

1. Excellent packaging for business owners.
2. Process-first rather than tool-first narrative.
3. Low-friction first implementation.
4. Software + enablement bundle.
5. Strong distribution through the Bilhon/Finch ecosystem.
6. Quick-win case construction.
7. Pricing understandable to SMB/midmarket buyers.
8. Broad horizontal catalog.

## Publicly unresolved / unproven areas

The public materials reviewed do not establish in detail:
- agent identity model;
- action authorization model;
- end-to-end audit model;
- eval framework;
- systematic shadow testing;
- versioned promotion gates;
- lineage of model-generated claims;
- enterprise ontology;
- data reconciliation layer;
- outcome attribution methodology;
- fine-grained policy enforcement;
- agent kill/revocation lifecycle;
- cross-agent governance;
- model challenger benchmarks.

Absence of public evidence does NOT prove the product lacks these capabilities.

## FLOOOW interpretation

ARKOM demonstrates an important market truth:

Companies do not primarily buy "models".
They buy operational capacity and measurable outcomes.

FLOOOW should learn from that commercial translation.
