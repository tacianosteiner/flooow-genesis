# ImportYeti — trade-document corroboration assessment

Status: **research only; no integration, API use, credential use or implementation authority**. Reviewed: 2026-10-12.

## Verified public product surface

ImportYeti's [API documentation](https://docs.importyeti.com/) shows US import Bills of Lading with importer/company, overseas supplier, product description, HS code, arrival date, port and weight. Its [API reference](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/reference) offers company and supplier search/profiles, product-keyword → supplier and buyer rankings, and PowerQuery routes for US import/export BOLs and Mexico import/export customs declarations. [PowerQuery syntax](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/powerquery) documents boolean/wildcard/range filters, dates and pagination. [Supplier ranking](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/reference/v1.0-product-product-suppliers) and [US buyer ranking](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/reference/v1.0-product-product-companies) address both sides of a product-keyword trade relationship. [US import supplier filters](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/reference/v1.0-powerquery-us-import-suppliers) include weight and TEU; company profiles expose HS and shipment summaries. These are documented fields and routes, not a quality or completeness audit.

The [getting-started guide](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/getting-started) says API keys must remain private and describes a product as goods imported into the US. The API is credit-metered; no paid call or credential was used. Its [Mexico import declaration endpoint](https://docs.importyeti.com/docs/reference/v1.0-powerquery-mx-import-declarations-declarationid) supplies documented declaration-level fields. Current docs list US and Mexico datasets; **Brazil import-declaration coverage was not evidenced in the reviewed reference**. No inference about all possible private offerings follows from that gap.

## FLOOOW fit and failure modes

Potential use: a candidate product keyword and independently resolved supplier identity can lead to shipment chronology, overseas supplier ↔ US buyer relationships, destination/route, HS and quantity/weight evidence. Shipment growth might corroborate a social/live signal when dates, description and identity align. It does **not** by itself prove that a live-shown product is the shipped SKU, that a listed supplier is the actual manufacturer, that a shipment sold through, that Brazilian demand exists, or that contribution margin is positive. Bills of Lading and customs declarations are documentary trade observations; creator metrics are a separate evidence class.

The first experiment should sample product/HS/description matches blind to the social signal, then inspect false matches, importer/supplier name ambiguity, freight forwarders, coverage bias, document lag and revision. Record exact BOL/declaration provenance and license constraints. Product-keyword rankings and relevance scores are discovery aids, never identity or supplier validation. Shipments can support a `PARTIAL` supplier relationship; actual factory status requires independent verification.

Provider integration is preferable to replicating trade-data collection **only if** licensed coverage, provenance, permitted retention/derivation, geography, update lag, cost and privacy meet a separate governance contract. The reviewed public docs do not establish FLOOOW's contractual right to store, redistribute, join or expose those records. Legal/licensing review and vendor terms are required before any integration. The present research creates no provider connection, data ingestion or authority.
